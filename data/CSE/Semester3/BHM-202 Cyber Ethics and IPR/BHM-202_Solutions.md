### Section A: Definitions & Concept Questions

**Q1. [Unit I | Topic: Information Technology Act 2000 | Type: Theory | Difficulty: Basic]**  
**Question:** What are the main aims and objectives of the Information Technology Act 2000?  

*Concept from scratch:* The Information Technology Act 2000 is a landmark legislation in India aimed at providing legal recognition for electronic transactions and addressing cybercrime. The main aims and objectives include:

1. To facilitate electronic governance by promoting the use of electronic records and digital signatures.

2. To enhance the legal framework for the validation of electronic contracts and transactions.

3. To provide for the prevention and punishment of cybercrime.

4. To promote the growth of e-commerce and secure the use of information technology.

5. To establish a framework for the appointment of an authority to monitor and regulate cyber activities.

*Step 1 — Identification of aims:* The aims include promoting e-governance and securing electronic transactions.  

*Step 2 — Identification of objectives:* Objectives include legal recognition of electronic documents, prevention of cybercrime, and promotion of e-commerce.  

*Result:* The main aims and objectives of the Information Technology Act 2000 are to facilitate electronic governance, enhance legal recognition of electronic documents, prevent cybercrime, and promote e-commerce.

**Q2. [Unit I | Topic: Cybercrime | Type: Theory | Difficulty: Basic]**  
**Question:** Define cybercrime and provide examples of different types of cybercrime.  

*Concept from scratch:* Cybercrime refers to illegal activities conducted via the internet or through the use of computer systems. It encompasses a wide range of offenses that can be classified into various categories based on their nature and impact.  

*Step 1 — Definition of cybercrime:* Cybercrime is any criminal activity that involves a computer, networked device, or a network.  

*Step 2 — Examples of cybercrime:*

1. **Hacking:** Unauthorized access to computer systems.

2. **Phishing:** Fraudulent attempts to acquire sensitive information by masquerading as a trustworthy entity.

3. **Malware Distribution:** Spreading harmful software that can damage systems or steal information.

4. **Identity Theft:** Stealing personal information to impersonate someone for financial gain.

*Result:* Cybercrime includes offenses like hacking, phishing, malware distribution, and identity theft.

### Section B: Computational & Applied Problems

**Q1. [Unit I | Topic: Jurisdiction | Type: Theory | Difficulty: Intermediate]**  
**Question:** Discuss the jurisdiction issues related to cybercrimes in India. Provide a case study that illustrates these issues.  

*Concept from scratch:* Jurisdiction in cybercrime cases refers to the legal authority of a court to hear and decide on cases involving cyber offenses. Given the borderless nature of the internet, jurisdictional challenges often arise when crimes are committed online.  

*Step 1 — Explanation of jurisdiction issues:* These issues include:

1. **Location of Crime:** Determining where the crime occurred when it spans multiple locations.

2. **Location of Victim and Perpetrator:** The geographic locations of the victim and perpetrator complicate legal proceedings.

3. **International Jurisdiction:** Different countries have different laws regarding cybercrimes, making enforcement challenging.

*Step 2 — Case study:* A relevant case is the "Shreya Singhal vs. Union of India" where the Supreme Court of India discussed the applicability of Section 66A of the IT Act concerning jurisdictional issues arising from online speech.

*Result:* Jurisdiction issues in cybercrimes in India involve determining the location of the crime, the geographic locations of the victim and perpetrator, and various international laws, as illustrated by the Shreya Singhal case.

**Q2. [Unit III | Topic: Copyright | Type: Theory | Difficulty: Basic]**  
**Question:** What are the main provisions of the Copyright Act in India?  

*Concept from scratch:* The Copyright Act in India governs the protection of literary, dramatic, musical, and artistic works, as well as cinematographic films and sound recordings.  

*Step 1 — Key provisions:*

1. **Rights of Authors:** Authors have the exclusive right to reproduce, distribute, and publicly perform their works.

2. **Duration of Copyright:** Copyright protection lasts for the lifetime of the author plus 60 years.

3. **Fair Use Doctrine:** Certain uses of copyrighted works are allowed without permission, such as for criticism, comment, news reporting, teaching, scholarship, or research.

*Result:* The main provisions of the Copyright Act in India include protection of authors' rights, duration of copyright, and the fair use doctrine.

### Section B: Computational & Applied Problems

**Q1. [Unit IV | Topic: Copyrights vs Patent | Type: Theory | Difficulty: Intermediate]**  
**Question:** Compare and contrast copyrights and patents, highlighting their key differences and applications.  

*Concept from scratch:* Copyrights and patents are both forms of intellectual property protection, but they apply to different types of creations and have distinct characteristics.  

*Step 1 — Definition and scope:*

1. **Copyright:** Protects original works of authorship, such as literature, music, and art, granting exclusive rights to reproduce and distribute.

2. **Patent:** Protects inventions or discoveries, giving the inventor exclusive rights to make, use, and sell the invention for a limited time.

*Step 2 — Key differences:*

1. **Duration:** Copyright lasts for the life of the author plus 60 years, while patents typically last for 20 years from the filing date.

2. **Subject Matter:** Copyright protects creative expressions, whereas patents protect functional inventions.

*Result:* Copyrights protect creative works and last for the author's life plus 60 years, while patents protect inventions and last for 20 years, with distinct subject matters and applications.

### Section C: Advanced Theory & Numericals

**Q1. [Unit IV | Topic: Domain Name Registration | Type: Theory | Difficulty: Advanced]**  
**Question:** Discuss the challenges and legal issues related to domain name registration in the context of trademark law.  

*Concept from scratch:* Domain name registration involves the allocation of web addresses, which can sometimes conflict with existing trademarks, leading to legal disputes.  

*Step 1 — Challenges of domain name registration:*

1. **Trademark Infringement:** There are instances of individuals registering domain names that are identical or similar to existing trademarks, leading to confusion and disputes.

2. **Cybersquatting:** Some registrants buy domain names with the intent to sell them at a profit to the trademark owners, creating legal and ethical dilemmas.

*Step 2 — Legal framework:*

1. **Uniform Domain Name Dispute Resolution Policy (UDRP):** A mechanism that allows trademark owners to challenge and resolve disputes over domain names.

2. **Anti-cybersquatting Consumer Protection Act (ACPA):** A law that provides remedies for trademark owners against cybersquatters.

*Result:* Challenges and legal issues in domain name registration include trademark infringement and cybersquatting, addressed through policies like UDRP and laws like ACPA.
