Hello friends, welcome to Gate Smashers. In today's video we are going to discuss CSMA, that is Carrier Sense Multiple Access. In MAC layer, after Aloha, we have the next protocol, that is CSMA. And the most important protocol, talking about competitive exams or talking from the point of view of college/University level, CSMA is most important. 

The name itself suggests Carrier Sense Multiple Access. Multiple Access means we are talking about a shared mode; shared means our medium, our channel is shared. Multiple nodes share the channel. Now, when I have to transmit some data, any node—let's say node A—wants to transmit data, we generally say that you can transmit data directly, like we saw in Pure Aloha and Slotted Aloha. But here we have an important concept: Carrier Sense. Before data transmission, the important step you have to do is to sense the carrier. The data you have to send, you keep it on hold; first, your transmitter, the node, the transmitter you are connected to, your transmitter will check the common medium, will sense it whether already some data is moving or not, if already some frequency, some signal is going or not. If already a signal is moving, then it means that medium is busy. So if your medium or channel is busy, then you hold your data. If you will also transmit, then obviously what will happen? Collision will occur. And if collision occurs, then your bandwidth will be wasted, so the throughput will go down. So this is the concept here, that when in a system, any node starts transmission, before starting transmission it will sense the carrier. 

Now, an important point here is students get confused: sensing the carrier doesn't mean that it will sense the entire channel, the entire medium, no. Let's say if our medium is 500 meters—our wire is 500 meters, half a kilometer—and it has multiple nodes, let's say 5 nodes are connected. So if A wants to send data, when A will sense, it will sense only this point, only at this point it will check: is data already moving? Is there already a signal? If already a signal is there, then A will not transmit data. But students get confused that it will sense the entire channel, no. If data is already moving here, it will not reach you because of propagation delay; already some node has transmitted data, but that signal has not yet reached you, so in that case you will not come to know. So this is the important point: to sense the carrier means you won't sense the entire path, only yours. You are standing in your house, in front of your house is any vehicle moving or not, you only have to check that. You can't check the vehicles on the whole street. When that vehicle comes in front of your house, you will know that there is a vehicle already, then you don't bring your car out. This is actually the concept of Carrier Sense. 

It has different variations, like 1-Persistent, 0-Persistent, and P-Persistent. 

1-Persistent means what? Whichever channel, whichever node—like if we say we have 5 nodes connected. Now in this whole medium, 1-Persistent means, let's say A has to transmit data, A already has data, but it will first sense if already a signal is moving or not. If no signal is moving, then A will immediately transmit the data, simple funda. If it gets that the medium is idle, nothing is moving, then A will immediately transmit the data. But if A senses and already a signal is moving here, then A will not transmit its data. However, 1-Persistent means it will continuously sense; it will continuously keep sensing till it gets idle. It is utilizing its resources continuously and checking if any data is still moving. While continuously sensing, the point where it doesn't get data—meaning it doesn't get a signal—it will immediately transmit the data. So this is the concept in 1-Persistent in which it will continuously sense, if it finds idle, then it will immediately put data, but if it finds busy, then in case of busy, it is continuously sensing, utilizing its resources. Generally, the concept of Ethernet works on this, but it has a problem if we see in the worst case. 

Worst case means, let's say A and D are communicating with each other, already data is moving, and in between are nodes N1, N2, and N3. Let's say all 3 nodes have data to transmit. In the worst case, all 3 have data, all 3 check at the same time, they are getting a signal. So they are waiting, but continuously checking: is it free now? Is it free now? Let's say when it is free, meaning data transmission has stopped; when N1 sensed free, N2 sensed free, N3 sensed free, then in case of 1-Persistent, if it is free, they will immediately transmit their data. Then what will happen? Collision will occur. So there are high chances of collision here. Why? Because in the worst case, when all got free, immediately they transmit data. 

Next, we have 0-Persistent. Persistent means, if we say in Hindi, persistent means tenacious, which is continuously checking. 0-Persistent means that it is a little less... Less means when it checks the signal, when it checks the medium, if the medium is idle, then in case of idle it has to do nothing, it will immediately transmit data. But if A checks the medium and already a signal is moving, meaning your medium is busy, if your medium is busy then A will do what? I will wait for some random amount of time. Some random amount of time means that any backoff time it will wait, and after the end of that time, it will start its transmission. Although it is also possible that let's say it takes 1 hour to wait, but the signal finished in half an hour, then it will wait half an hour extra. 

So in the worst case, you can say, that the same concept we take here: if there are N1, N2, N3 and if A and D are talking to each other, signal is already moving, then N1, N2, N3 feel the signal is busy, meaning the medium is busy. But as soon as transmission is over, the channel gets free. Now N1 does what? When it first feels busy, it will wait for a random amount of time. Let's say it thinks "I'll wait for 5 minutes," N2 thinks "I'll wait for 15 minutes," and N3 thinks "I'll wait for 30 minutes." So here, after 5 minutes N1 will start its work, its transmission; at that time N2 will not do, N3 will not do. So collision here, compared to 1-Persistent, it will be less. 

But if we talk about the best case, in a normal case A and D were talking to each other, A stops its transmission, N1 sees that the signal is already moving and it sets its timer that "I am waiting for 30 seconds or 30 minutes." It is possible that your channel becomes free before, and you set the time that "I will check transmission 1 hour later, after 1 hour can I transmit or not." But it is possible that the next second the medium gets free. So your transmission that will start or initialize, that will obviously be late. This is a problem in 0-Persistent, but collision as compared to 1-Persistent will be less. 

Then P-Persistent. P-Persistent means P here is the probability value. P-Persistent means what? It's the same concept that if we have multiple nodes, and those nodes are continuously checking. This is a hybrid approach, a mixture of these two is P-Persistent. It is continuously checking. When it gets idle, it will immediately put its data. But if it doesn't get idle, the channel is busy, then it will sense continuously like 1-Persistent. It keeps sensing continuously. Let's say it gets the medium free. After getting the medium free, it has $P$ probability that it will start its transmission. Meaning after finding the channel free, it will not transmit data immediately; the probability value is $P$ and on that basis it will start its transmission. 

The value of $P$, generally the value of probability, is between 0 and 1. Because if it is 0, then it becomes this [0-Persistent]; if the value is 1, then it becomes this [1-Persistent]. So P-Persistent means an intermediate value. You can make the probability value 0.5 or 0.1, by which higher throughput will be achieved, and collision will be less. The concept of WiFi generally uses P-Persistent.
