Hello friends, welcome to Gate Smashers. Today we are going to discuss circuit switching. Circuit switching is very important from a GATE and UGC NET point of view, and in this video, I am going to discuss facts that will help you a lot in the exam.

First of all, circuit switching was generally designed for telephone networks. At the time it was designed, there was no concept of OSI layers and TCP models. Today, we are using computer networks where computers are connected within a network to share some data. We might have personal computers, laptops, and many other devices in a computer network. However, earlier, the main network was the telephone network. To physically connect devices in the telephone network, we used the circuit switching technique. 

If you look at the diagram here, you have a phone exchanger. You obviously know how landlines work; the telephone exchanges are connected to each other. If a user wants to talk to any other person, what do they have to do to set up the connection? We pick up the receiver, dial the number, and a tone goes off, which is a kind of connection setup. Whenever we talk about how the connection is made here, the first step is setup time. In GATE and UGC, theory-based questions or numericals might be asked. We are going to discuss the theory parts first.

I have written some very important points here. First of all, circuit switching works on the physical layer. When we talk in layers, data comes from the application layer, goes to the network layer, and comes to the data link layer. There is no such layer concept here. By data, we mean we picked up the receiver, established the connection, and after that, we started talking in a continuous flow. In a way, the connection has to be made at the physical layer, and the devices are connected. 

Let us see how it is connected here. T1 is already connected to a telephone exchange. Our landline telephone is connected to the telephone exchange inside the city, and that telephone exchange is connected with the telephone exchange of another city, which is already connected to the person we have to talk to. The fund is very simple: if T1 wants to talk to T5, the device here is working like a multiplexer where many people can connect from here, and there is a wire going from there. The connection is established in this way. 

This means the circuit is already made. To connect the circuit, one port is connected from here and one port is connected from there, creating a complete dedicated path. This dedicated path is used to communicate. Always remember that circuit switching uses a dedicated path. A dedicated path means these people are not connected point-to-point directly; it just looks like they are connected dedicatedly because once the connection is made, you can keep talking on the phone as long as you want until you disconnect. You can flow the data.

The second point is contiguous flow. Continuous flow means once the connection is made and you start talking, you will continue to have a contiguous data flow. When we talk inside layered networks, we say that data comes from the application layer above, is divided into segments, segments into packets, and packets into frames. There is no such thing in circuit switching. You start talking directly, and all the data will flow contiguously. 

Here is an important point that has a high chance of appearing in the exam: if T1 is sending data to T5, can that data go out of order? Out of order means packet 1, packet 2, packet 3—supposing I am sending 3 packets, P1, P2, P3—could P3 reach first, then P2, then P1? No, it cannot be out of order. It will always go in the correct order because if you are talking on the phone and say "hello, how are you?", "how are you" will not go first; "hello" will always go first. The line moves in one flow.

The third point is headers. Why do we use headers? With data, we have to explain addresses, meaning our source address and destination address. Like in the network layer, we talk about source IP and destination IP; in the transport layer, we say source port and destination port; and in MAC, we use source MAC and destination MAC addresses. Here, you do not need any address and there is no need for headers. As soon as you have setup—which is the advantage of the setup, although setup is time-consuming—once the connection is made, you do not need to put any headers. The data will flow properly because what you have done in the setup is made a reservation. 

Reservation in the setup means you have saved this port (it is yours till you terminate the connection), you have reserved the bandwidth of this link and channel, and you have reserved the buffer and resources. Now, no one else is using the resource anymore; you are using it alone. Because of this, there is no need for headers here. Packets will keep going in one line without any header, and I do not have to tell my address or the destination. Addresses are needed when someone takes the data on the way and decides how to send it, but there is nothing like that here. The end-to-end connection is already made dedicatedly.

If we talk about efficiency, the efficiency of circuit switching is less. Why is it less? Because of the same reservation. When we have reserved the resources, it means that after the resources are reserved, some other person cannot share them. Suppose you are talking on the phone and you say, "Wait for two minutes, I will complete my TV serial, after that I will talk to you." You did not hang up the phone; you put the receiver on the side. The connection is still on, and that reservation and bandwidth are still reserved by you. But are you sending data? What are you talking? No. You are sitting unnecessarily and reserving the resources, and because of that, efficiency gets reduced.

After that, let us look at delay. In circuit switching, delay is less. What delay are we talking about? The delay of nodes coming in the way. There is no delay of nodes in the way because when we said that the packet comes out from this port to this port, no one will stop this packet in between. As we will discuss further when we talk about packet switching or message switching, in those methods, intermediate devices like routers store those packets, do some processing, and forward them. There is no method of store-and-forward here. As soon as you start talking, no one will store and forward it on the way; it will keep running in one flow, and no one will stop it. Due to this, the delay is less.

However, what is the problem here? Because it is working on the physical layer, we cannot send data by dividing it into packets, and more importantly, efficiency is low. That is why circuit switching is best for telephone networks. But if we talk about messaging, email, WhatsApp, or computer networks in general, you cannot use this technology there.

Finally, you can get numerical questions about the total time. If you are asked how much time it took, the total time is the sum of:
1. Setup time: The time it takes to make a reservation (which cannot be calculated from basic formulas, so if you get a numerical, it must be given to you).
2. Transmission time: The message size divided by the bandwidth.
3. Propagation delay: The distance over velocity (speed).
4. Tear-down time: The time taken to terminate the connection and free all resources.

In cases where circuit switching is used, this will be the total time formula. So these are the major concepts and points related to circuit switching.
