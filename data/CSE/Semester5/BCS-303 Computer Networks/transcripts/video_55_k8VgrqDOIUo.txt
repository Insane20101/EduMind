"A datagram of 3000 bytes, which contains 20 bytes of IP header and 2980 bytes of IP payload, reached a router and must be forwarded to a link with an MTU of 500 bytes. How many fragments will be generated? Also write the MF, offset, and total length values for all."

This type of question often comes from fragmentation from the network layer. Questions come mainly from the network layer, either from classful or classless addressing, and the other topic is related to fragmentation. And where does this topic come from? From the IPv4 header format. 

So what do we do in IPv4? We have a header of a minimum of 20 bytes, and what is the remaining one? The IP payload. What is payload? Pure data which comes to us from the upper layer. 

So what scenario do we have? There is a router, and the router has received data from somewhere. A datagram came; that datagram is of 3000 bytes, out of which 20 bytes is the header and the remaining 2980 bytes is the payload, meaning data. Now this router has to send this data to any link. That link is connected to any network, and to that link you cannot send data of more than 500 bytes. It means that link can’t handle data of more than 500 bytes. Or you can say like this also: when you send data of more than 500 bytes from this link, the network ahead—the data link layer of that network, where we say MTU—the data link layer can’t accept data of more than 500 bytes. 

So what do we have to do? The router can’t send data of 3000 bytes directly. So what will the router do at its end? Fragmentation. And this only we have to find out in the question: how many fragments will be formed? What will be their values?

So guys, let’s start here. First of all, how many fragments will be formed? You will have to find this first. Why did we add the header to all these packets? Because IPv4 is a datagram service. What is datagram service?
