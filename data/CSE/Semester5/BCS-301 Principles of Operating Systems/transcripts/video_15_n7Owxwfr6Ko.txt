Burst time... that's the 2nd time. It is the time required by a process to get executed on a CPU. Meaning, this is the time duration. It's the time duration required by a process to get executed on the CPU. Arrival time means the time when the process arrived. Let's discuss it with a simple example. Second is burst time... burst time means how much time I'll take in depositing the money. Completion time is not the duration; completion time is also a point of time. This is actually a very simple concept, but many students make mistakes in this concept. So that's why always remember that burst time is a duration, but arrival time and completion time are not a duration; they are a point of time at which a process gets completed. 

After that comes the turnaround time. This is a very important portion here, because you get a lot of questions on CPU scheduling in GATE and other competitive exams where we use different CPU scheduling algorithms. And you get asked how much is the turnaround time or waiting time. So the simple formula of turnaround time is completion time minus arrival time. 

Turnaround time means the whole time. Meaning, I entered in the bank at 11 o'clock. I know that it'll take 15 mins in depositing the money, but I came out of the bank at 12 o'clock. So it means turnaround time is 12 minus 11, i.e., 1. Meaning, it took me a whole 1 hour, i.e., I've taken 60 mins. So that 60 mins is the turnaround time. 

Now you must be thinking in your mind that depositing money was supposed to be done in 15 mins, so what did I do there for 60 mins? Let's discuss about it also. 

Next is the waiting time. Waiting time means turnaround time minus burst time. You have to keep these formulas in mind, but you don't have to cram it. Waiting time means we calculate the turnaround time that at 12, at 12 o'clock I came out of the bank, and I went in at 11 o'clock.
