Process synchronization is a very main topic of operating system. In process synchronization, if multiple processes runs in our system then there could be two modes, one is serial mode and the other is parallel mode. Serial mode means processes will get executed one by one. When one process got started, other process can't start until it won't get terminated. 

It is like an ATM. When customers use ATM machines, they are using that one by one. Nobody else can enter until one person's transaction does not end. This way, when we execute processes, then there is no problem in it because one running process won't make any effect on the other. 

However, if we talk about parallel execution, our system these days uses a multiprogrammed environment and a time-sharing environment. We can't give a guarantee that there'll be one process at a time. A lot of processes can run simultaneously. When this many processes run at a time, then we get a very big problem that there could be some processes which are cooperative and others could be independent. 

I am categorizing the processes into two types: cooperative processes and independent processes. Independent processes mean execution of one is not putting any effect on the other. There is nothing common. For example, if I am doing a transaction today on SBI's online server, and another transaction I am doing on HDFC's server, there is nothing common between them; both are getting executed on different servers. Those are independent processes, so there's no benefit to studying about them because they are getting executed differently, so there's nothing common between them. 

Cooperative processes are those processes where execution of one affects the other. The important point is why we call them cooperative and how the execution of one affects the other. The reason is that they share something. There is something common between them. 

Now, what could that common thing be? The common element could be a variable, meaning two processes are sharing something with each other, i.e., a variable. They are using a common variable. They can share the memory or buffer; when two processes are running at a time, maybe they are sharing the same memory or buffer. The third can be code; there could be some code or coding that is common between both. One more thing can be resources. In resources, we are having a CPU, printer, scanner, and various other hardware. 

If there is anything common between them—whether it's a variable, memory, code, or resources—we call those types of processes cooperative processes. Here is a very crucial point: if they share anything common, then the execution of one can affect the other. 

For example, in IRCTC, thousands of people can do transactions at a time. Even in our laptops, PCs, and mobiles, there are many processes at a time which can share something common. We call those types of processes cooperative processes. 

Now, our topic is process synchronization. Why is there a need for process synchronization here? If we won't properly synchronize these cooperative processes, then they can create a problem. It's not always mandatory that they will create problems, but yes, they can create problems. 

We will see an example here. It is just like a simple example: if I would give a guitar to someone who doesn't have any knowledge about music, then he'll create noise rather than music. Why? If cooperative processes won't get executed properly, then it'll create some problem here; it'll lead to some inconsistent or deadlock state that it'd be very difficult to come out of it. So that's why it's very important to synchronize them.
