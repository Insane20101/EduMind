The degree of multi-level programming is the number of processes present in RAM. Meaning, if I bring more and more processes inside RAM, it directly increases CPU utilization. If you bring more and more processes inside RAM... this is a RAM and there is a limitation in the size of RAM. The size of RAM is limited. 

If I bring only two or three processes inside RAM, the problem is sometimes processes demand input-output operations. As soon as those processes demand input-output operations, according to the process state, we put them in the block state. At that time, the CPU becomes idle. Throughput will decrease as soon as the CPU becomes idle, meaning the performance will start degrading. So, we have to keep the CPU busy all the time. To keep the CPU busy and to bring maximum utilization, I have to bring more and more processes inside RAM. 

But RAM is limited, so I can't bring the whole process inside RAM. Here we use the concept of paging. In paging, what we do is we divide processes into pages, then put them inside RAM. This means if I have a lot of processes, let's say I have 100 processes, first, we will divide 100 processes into pages, and we will bring those pages inside RAM. 

Here is how to maximize the degree of multi-programming. The funda is simple: how to maximize the degree of multi-programming? If I bring only one page of each and every process inside RAM, meaning whatever my processes are, the maximum number of processes involved will be within RAM. This means all the processes will feel that we are present inside RAM. But the entire process is not inside RAM; we bring only one or two pages of it inside RAM. I am not bringing the whole process; I am taking only a 10% or 20% portion of a process inside RAM. So I have an advantage with that, whenever the CPU will ask for any particular process or data, I will give that page to the CPU. 

But what is the problem here? The worst-case scenario could be, let's say, I brought page number 1 of Process P1 inside RAM, page number 1 of Process P2 inside RAM, then page number 1 of Process P3 inside RAM, page number 1 of Process P4 inside RAM, let's say page number 1 of Process P100 inside RAM. This helped me achieve one thing, and that is the degree of multi-programming. Meaning, I tried to bring maximum processes inside RAM. 

But what is the problem with it? Is the entire process of P1 inside RAM? No! Only one page of it is present in RAM. If the CPU is asking for page 1 of process P1, then there is no problem. There is also no problem with page 1 of process P2 either. No problem with page 1 of process P3. But in case the CPU asks for page 2 of process P1, page 2 of the process P1 is not present anywhere, so this will be called a page fault. 

And whenever a page fault occurs, we use page fault service time. I mean to say that we will bring the page from the hard disk into the main memory, and it takes a lot of time. I have already explained this topic—what is the page fault service time in virtual memory?—so you can follow that video.

Meaning, I have brought more and more pages of processes inside RAM, but whatever is the demand of the CPU, that page is not present inside the RAM. 

So as soon as the pages will not be present inside RAM, we call it a page fault. When we service a page fault, it takes a lot of time. The entire operating system will get busy in servicing page faults. Obviously, due to this, the performance of the operating system will degrade.
