What is the reason? In a procedural or formal query language, whichever programmer or user is writing the query, they have to mention two things: one is what to do, and the other one is how to do. What is the meaning of this—what to do and how to do? Why are we using relational algebra? To access the database. My database is lying in the back end, and the users are trying to access data from different areas. Now, to access that data, what is the first point? What to do—actually, what to do to access that data? But how to access that data? For that, what is the method? What is the procedure? That you have to mention in the query. 

This means relational algebra is like C programming; we have to mention what we actually want to do, and how to do that work—that is what we have to mention, both things. Therefore, actually, the meaning of a procedural language is this.
