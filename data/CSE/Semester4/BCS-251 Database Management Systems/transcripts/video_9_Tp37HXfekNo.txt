Welcome, friends, to Gate Smashers. In this video, we are going to discuss what a primary key is. In a Database Management System (DBMS), the concept of keys is extremely important. Whether you are talking about competitive exams, college or university-level exams, or even placements, questions about keys are certainly asked. And that does not mean just one key, like asking what a primary key is or what a candidate key is, and thinking that is enough. No. If you talk about normalization or SQL, the concept of keys is used everywhere. So, in this video, I am focusing specifically on the primary key. 

What is the story of the primary key? What is it, and how do we talk about it? I am starting this story with a simple example. Let us say we have 100 students in a class, and I need to store their data. Obviously, I will not always call out 100 students by face if I have to take attendance. There is an online system. So, what would I do in the online system? I will save the details of all those students. I will make an Excel file in which I register the student's registration number, roll number, father's name, mother's name, student's name, age, gender, marks, address, phone number, and Aadhaar card number, making multiple attributes. I will make a table within which multiple attributes will be defined, and then we will fill in the details of all the students. In a way, you can visualize that there is a table in which we have made all the entries. 

Now, here comes the concept of the key. First of all, what is the meaning of a key? Consider how we have a key for a lock. What is its property? Every lock has its unique key, and that key will open only that lock. I am talking about the best case here; I am not talking about normal cases. A lot can happen in India—maybe you can open multiple locks with the same key, but I am not talking about that. Nor am I talking about a master key roaming around with thieves. What is the normal concept of a key? There is only one key for a lock, and from that, I can only open that lock. The same thing we are doing here, meaning you are uniquely identifying something. If two things are the same, how do you identify that you are different and another is different? Through the key. 

Now, talking about a database, how will we do this? You have attributes in the database. If I say "name"—if we take the student's name—is the student's name guaranteed to be different? No. Check for yourself; you will have a lot of kids in your class or college whose names, even if you look at the first and last name, will be the same. You cannot guarantee it. What about age or date of birth? There are 365 days in a year, or 366 in a leap year, so obviously somewhere the date of birth will be the same. Father's name can also be the same; mother's name can also be the same. In case two brothers and sisters or two brothers are studying together, obviously, their father and mother will be the same. Similarly, multiple attributes may have the same values. But which attributes cannot have the same value? That will be a unique key. 

Which attributes could these be? Let us say, your phone number. It will come to your mind that, sir, the phone number will be different. Fine, the phone number will always be different. The Aadhaar card number: yes, you can say that the Aadhaar cards we have, everyone's numbers will be different. PAN card as well—although I am talking about students, even if you do not take the PAN card into account, and even if you do, it will still be unique. In this way, even if you take the license number, it will be unique. Take the registration number or roll number, because in many universities there are registration numbers, and in many colleges there are roll numbers, so they will also be unique. So, in this way, you can count multiple attributes where, sir, all of these will be unique; there is no duplicacy in them. 

Now let us talk about what we call all these attributes. We call all these attributes candidate keys. What are all these attributes being made of? All of these are candidate keys. So, what is the property of a candidate keys? It is unique. So, uniqueness is achieved. But will uniqueness alone work? It cannot work. How? 

Let us suppose the phone number. I am assuming the phone number. If I want to find out about a student, I will fill in the phone number and the details will come up. So, is there any guarantee that every student must have given the correct phone number, or even given a phone number at all? This is also not guaranteed. And there is also a possibility that, let us suppose, if two brothers and sisters are studying together, both of them may have given the same number in the database. That is possible, isn't it? This case is possible. You can say that a student has not given a phone number, or it is also possible that if there are two siblings, somewhere they mentioned the same phone number, and the two of them do not have different phone numbers. 

Next, consider the Aadhaar card. Now you can also say that it is not necessary that every single student has applied for one. If a student has applied, then obviously their Aadhaar card will be unique. But what is the guarantee that if we talk about your college or university—let us suppose there are 10,000 to 20,000 students, or 50,000 students—can you give a guarantee that all students will have an Aadhaar card, all students will have a PAN card, or a license number? That is not a guarantee. And because of this, it can happen that if I am looking for a student—meaning if I entered their entry online—there are two or three entries corresponding to it, or no entries corresponding to it, because you did not have that data in your database. 

So what is the second property that you need here? That is "not null." It could also be possible that I had not given a phone number. I took admission in a university or a college, they asked for a phone number, and I did not give it yet or I gave a wrong number. So obviously somewhere I left this field empty—let us suppose we keep it null—or in the same sibling case, if you give the same phone number, it will pick the same phone number, and what will happen because of it? Tomorrow when you search for the student, there will not be one entry corresponding to it; there may be two or three entries. So it is not uniquely identified. 

The core requirement of a database is that I must be able to uniquely identify each one. I can uniquely identify every single tuple. If this is not achieved, when will it be achieved? First, it should be unique. Second, it should not be null. "Not null" means that you cannot leave this field empty. You cannot leave this field empty; you have to fill something in this field. Something has to be filled. 

Let us suppose if I talk about the phone number, the Aadhaar card, or the PAN card. Let us suppose a student comes to get admission in your college. You will definitely fill that field. Now, if you fill that field and the student says that they do not have an Aadhaar card number yet, what will you do? Will you fill any wrong value in that field, like "$" or "#," just to fill it? Because you cannot leave it empty—it is a not-null field—what will happen in that case? You are making a wrong entry in the database. 

What could be the second case? You will say, "If you want to take admission, give me the Aadhaar card number, because the Aadhaar card number will have to be filled, it is a not-null field." Null means empty; you cannot leave it null. What will you do then? You will say, "You will not get admission now. Bring the Aadhaar card number first." So, obviously, this case will not happen, because then competing colleges and universities will pull that student away. Similarly, if we talk about a PAN card number or a license number, you cannot make these a mandatory field. 

To whom would you apply a mandatory field in the case of a student? A registration number or a roll number. What is the actual concept of a primary key? A primary key is unique plus not null. So I got an advantage from the candidate key: uniqueness. It will always be unique. But out of all candidate keys, I am just taking the one that is the most appropriate. Appropriate means, like I just told you, in the case of a student, the most appropriate will be either the registration number or the roll number. If it is a small college, then you can also take the roll number. If it is a university, then what will you take? A registration number or a serial number as a primary key, because it is unique—since it is a candidate key, it is definitely unique—plus it is not null; you cannot leave it empty. 

So whenever you talk about this case, you can pick any database. In that database, your primary key is always unique plus not null. And second, we never take the value of the primary key from the users; we give the value of the primary key to the user. So let us suppose if you go to a college or university to take admission, you will not say, "Sir, I want this roll number or I want this registration number, I want a VIP registration number." There is no such thing. They will give you an automatically generated registration number or roll number, because this field cannot be left empty. 

Similarly, let us suppose if you go to the passport office. What will the passport number at the passport office be? It will be a primary key, because it is also unique plus not null. Now, in the passport office, data of the same students—sorry, of the same people—will be there, right? Those who have a passport, meaning those who have applied for a passport. Now, do you ask for the passport number by yourself? No. They will give you the passport number; that is the primary key. 

Similarly, in the license office, if you are applying for a license, then the license office will have the data of those people whose license has been applied for already, or who have already taken a proper or learning license. So the license number will also be provided to you by the office. Because this is a very, very tough field—it becomes strict somewhere—both unique and not null have to be achieved. Data should be unique and also not null, meaning you cannot even leave that field empty and you cannot fill duplicates into it. 

So this is the main concept of the primary key. Always remember: the primary key is always unique plus not null, but a candidate key is only unique. You can leave it empty, you can skip it; but if you do not want to skip it, then what will you make it? A primary key. 

So the last point here is: in a database, can more than one primary key—meaning two, three, or four—be possible? No, there must be only one primary key, because I told you first, making a primary key is very hard. You can neither leave it empty nor can you fill duplicates in it. So if you try to make more than one attribute the primary key, then at first, your database will not allow it. Any server or any software you are using—whether you are using Oracle, SQL Server, IBM—none of them will allow you. Because why would I hire two people if my work is getting done by one person? The same thing applies here: if my work is getting done by one attribute, then I will not mess with multiple attributes. That is why your software does not even allow you to make more than one primary key. 

But yes, more than one candidate key can be possible, because multiple unique attributes can exist. Alone, "not null" is also possible; you can also make more than one attribute not null. But if we talk about them combined—unique and not null—it will be just one attribute, and we call it the primary key. That will depend on the database that you are creating in which scenario. In a student's case, a registration number or roll number will be made. In the case of an Aadhaar card, the Aadhaar card number will be made. In the case of PAN, a PAN number will be made. In the case of a passport, a passport number. In the case of a license, a license number. So whatever database you talk about, in those cases, you will understand what the concept of a primary key is. Thank you.
