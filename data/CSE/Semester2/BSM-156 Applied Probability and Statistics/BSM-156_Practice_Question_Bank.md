# UNIT-I: Basic Statistics & Curve Fitting

## Section A (2 Marks) — Basic

1. Define frequency distribution. Distinguish between discrete and continuous frequency distributions.

2. Define mean, median, and mode of a frequency distribution.

3. Write the empirical relationship between mean, median, and mode.

4. Define the r-th moment about the mean (central moment) of a distribution.

5. Define the r-th moment about an arbitrary point (raw moment).

6. Write the relation connecting the first four central moments to the raw moments (about an arbitrary origin).

7. Define the moment generating function (MGF) of a random variable.

8. State how the r-th moment about the mean can be obtained from the MGF.

9. Define skewness. What does positive and negative skewness indicate about a distribution?

10. Define Karl Pearson's coefficient of skewness.

11. Define the coefficient of skewness based on moments (β₁, γ₁).

12. Define kurtosis. Name the three types of kurtosis (leptokurtic, mesokurtic, platykurtic) with their β₂ values.

13. Define the method of least squares for curve fitting.

14. Write the normal equations for fitting a straight line y = a + bx by the method of least squares.

15. Write the normal equations for fitting a second-degree parabola y = a + bx + cx² by the method of least squares.

## Section B (7 Marks) — Intermediate

1. Calculate the mean, median, and mode for a given grouped frequency distribution.

2. The median of a distribution is given as 46. Find the missing frequencies f₁ and f₂ for the classes 30–40 and 50–60, given the total frequency and the remaining class frequencies.

3. The first four moments of a distribution about the value 5 are 2, 4, 10, and 25. Calculate the moments about the mean, and comment upon the skewness and kurtosis of the distribution.

4. Fit a straight line y = a + bx to the given data by the method of least squares, and estimate y for a specified value of x.

5. Fit a parabola of second degree (y = a + bx + cx²) to the given data using the method of least squares, and predict y for a specified value of x.

6. A simply supported beam carries a concentrated load P at its midpoint. Corresponding to various values of P, the maximum deflection Y is measured. Using the method of least squares, find a law of the type Y = a + bP from the given data.

7. Define skewness and kurtosis of a distribution. Given the first four moments of a distribution, find the coefficient of skewness and kurtosis.

8. Calculate Karl Pearson's coefficient of skewness for a given grouped frequency distribution, and interpret the result.

9. Fit a straight line of the form y = a + bx to a given dataset and comment on the goodness of fit.

10. For a symmetric distribution with standard deviation 5, find the value of the fourth central moment required for the distribution to be (i) leptokurtic (ii) mesokurtic.

## Section C (10 Marks) — Advanced

1. Fit a parabola y = a + bx + cx² to the given data using the method of least squares, and predict y at a specified value of x.
   **OR**
   Define skewness and kurtosis of a distribution. The first four moments of a distribution are 0, 2.5, 0.7, and 18.71. Find the coefficient of skewness and kurtosis, and comment on the nature of the distribution.

2. Derive the normal equations for fitting a straight line y = a + bx by the method of least squares, and apply them to a given dataset.
   **OR**
   The first n moments about an arbitrary point are given. Derive the formulas expressing the central moments (moments about the mean) in terms of these raw moments, up to the fourth order.

3. Calculate the mean, median, mode, and the coefficients of skewness and kurtosis for a large grouped dataset, and comment comprehensively on the shape of the distribution.
   **OR**
   Explain the significance of the moment generating function. Derive the MGF of a discrete random variable from first principles and show how it generates the moments about the origin.

# UNIT-II: Correlation and Regression

## Section A (2 Marks) — Basic

1. Define correlation. Distinguish between positive, negative, and zero correlation with examples.

2. Define the Karl Pearson coefficient of correlation. Write its formula.

3. Define Spearman's rank correlation coefficient. Write its formula for the case of no ties.

4. Write the modified formula for Spearman's rank correlation coefficient when ties are present.

5. Define regression. Distinguish between the line of regression of y on x and of x on y.

6. Write the two regression equations in terms of the regression coefficients b_yx and b_xy.

7. Write the relation between the correlation coefficient r and the two regression coefficients b_xy and b_yx.

8. Write the basic difference between correlation and regression.

9. State the property that the product of the two regression coefficients cannot exceed 1.

10. Define the coefficient of determination (r²) and state its significance.

11. State the condition under which the two regression lines coincide.

12. Distinguish between linear and non-linear (curvilinear) regression.

## Section B (7 Marks) — Intermediate

1. Calculate Karl Pearson's coefficient of correlation between two given variables X and Y from raw data, and interpret the result.

2. Find the means of X and Y and the coefficient of correlation between them from the two regression equations 2Y−X−50=0 and 3Y−2X−10=0.

3. Obtain the rank correlation coefficient for a given set of paired ranks/scores (with repeated ranks/ties present).

4. The data below gives the marks obtained by several students in two subjects. Determine whether there is a connection between the marks obtained in the two subjects, using an appropriate correlation measure.

5. Find the equations of the lines of regression of x on y and y on x for a given bivariate dataset.

6. If the regression lines of y on x and x on y are given as y = −0.50x+11.64 and x = −0.87y+19.13 respectively, find (i) the means of x and y, (ii) the correlation coefficient between x and y.

7. Find the coefficient of correlation from a given dataset of paired (x, y) observations using the direct (product-moment) formula.

8. Discuss regression and its importance in statistical analysis. From given data, find the equations of the lines of regression of x on y and y on x.

9. Discuss the relationship between regression analysis and correlation analysis, highlighting how one can be derived from the other.

10. Given the variance of savings is one-quarter the variance of income for a family, and the coefficient of correlation is 0.4, with the regression of saving (x) on income (y) expressed as x = a + y/m, find the value of m.

## Section C (10 Marks) — Advanced

1. Discuss regression and its importance in detail. From the data given, find the equations of the lines of regression of x on y and y on x, and use them to estimate one variable given the other.
   **OR**
   Discuss the relation between regression analysis and correlation analysis. If the regression lines of y on x and x on y are given, find (i) the mean values of x and y, (ii) the correlation coefficient between x and y, and (iii) verify consistency of the two regression coefficients.

2. Calculate the coefficient of correlation between two variables from a large dataset, and separately compute the Spearman rank correlation coefficient for the same data. Compare and comment on the two results.
   **OR**
   Derive the formula relating the correlation coefficient r to the two regression coefficients b_xy and b_yx, and use it to show that 0 ≤ r² ≤ 1.

3. A study of two variables reveals a certain regression relationship of the form x = a + z/m (non-linear/transformed regression). Given the ratio of variances and the correlation coefficient, derive the value of the constant m, showing all algebraic steps.
   **OR**
   Explain, with derivation, why the two regression lines are generally different (except in the case of perfect correlation), and derive the angle of intersection between the two regression lines in terms of r, σₓ, σᵧ.

# UNIT-III: Probability

## Section A (2 Marks) — Basic

1. Define a random experiment, trial, and outcome, with an example.

2. Define an event. Distinguish between a simple (elementary) event and a compound event.

3. Define mutually exclusive events and exhaustive events, with examples.

4. Define independent events. How does independence differ from mutual exclusivity?

5. Define the sample space of a random experiment, with an example.

6. State the classical (a priori) definition of probability.

7. State the empirical (statistical/relative frequency) definition of probability.

8. State the addition theorem of probability for two events (both mutually exclusive and non-mutually-exclusive cases).

9. State the multiplication theorem of probability for two independent events, and for two dependent events.

10. Define conditional probability P(A|B), and write its formula.

11. State Bayes' theorem (statement only).

12. Define odds in favour of an event and odds against an event, in terms of probability.

## Section B (7 Marks) — Intermediate

1. An urn contains 12 pink balls and 6 blue balls. Without replacement, two balls are drawn one after another. What is the probability that both balls drawn are pink?

2. If A, B, C are mutually exclusive and exhaustive events such that P(B) = (3/2)P(A) and P(C) = (1/3)P(B), find the odds in favour of (A∪B).

3. In a town, it is rainy one third of the days. Given that it is rainy, there is heavy traffic with probability 1/2; given it is not rainy, heavy traffic occurs with probability 1/4. Given rain and heavy traffic, a person arrives late with probability 1/2; given no rain and no traffic, the probability of being late is 1/8; in other combinations it is 0.25. Find (i) the probability that it is not raining, there is heavy traffic, and the person is not late, (ii) the probability that the person is late.

4. Three bags contain 3 red & 7 black; 8 red & 2 black; and 4 red & 6 black balls respectively. One bag is selected at random and a ball is drawn from it. If the ball drawn is red, find the probability that it was drawn from the third bag.

5. Two cards are drawn at random without replacement from a pack of 52 playing cards. Find the probability that both cards are black, or both cards are spades.

6. A theatre chain studies its customers' spending; the distribution is approximately normal. Using conditional-probability style reasoning (or direct computation for later distribution use), discuss how such empirical data supports the classical/empirical probability definitions.

7. State and prove the addition theorem of probability for two events that are not mutually exclusive.

8. State and prove the multiplication theorem of probability for two dependent events, using the definition of conditional probability.

9. A committee of 9 students has 2 from first year, 3 from second year, and 4 from third year. Three students are removed at random. Find the probability that (i) all three belong to different classes, (ii) two belong to the same class and the third to a different class, (iii) all three belong to the same class.

10. There are two groups of subjects: one with 5 science and 3 engineering subjects, the other with 3 science and 5 engineering subjects. An unbiased die is cast; if 3 or 5 turns up, a subject is selected from the first group, otherwise from the second group. Find the probability that an engineering subject is ultimately selected.

## Section C (10 Marks) — Advanced

1. State and prove Baye's theorem. A can hit a target 4 times in 5 shots, B 3 times in 4 shots, C twice in 3 shots. They fire a volley. What is the probability that at least two shots hit the target?
   **OR**
   Three bags contain different compositions of red and black balls. One bag is selected at random and a ball drawn from it turns out to be red. Using Bayes' theorem, find the probability that it was drawn from each of the bags, and verify the probabilities sum to 1.

2. A committee of students from different years is to be formed/reduced by random removal. Derive, from first principles (using combinations), the probability that the resulting group satisfies a given class-distribution condition, for all sub-cases of the problem.
   **OR**
   There are two groups of subjects with given compositions. A random mechanism (die roll) selects which group to draw from. Derive, using the law of total probability, the overall probability that a subject of a specified type is selected, and extend the argument using Bayes' theorem to find the probability the die showed a specific outcome given the subject type selected.

3. A survey/experiment provides conditional probabilities of an event under several distinct conditions (e.g., weather and traffic conditions affecting lateness). Using the law of total probability and Bayes' theorem, find (i) the overall probability of the compound event, (ii) the conditional probability of one of the original conditions given the final observed outcome.
   **OR**
   State and prove the addition and multiplication theorems of probability for three events A, B, C, and use them to derive the general formula for P(A∪B∪C).

# UNIT-IV: Probability Distributions & Test of Significance

## Section A (2 Marks) — Basic

1. Define a discrete random variable and a continuous random variable, with examples.

2. Define the (cumulative) distribution function of a random variable, and state its properties.

3. Define the probability mass function (PMF) and probability density function (PDF).

4. Define the Binomial distribution and state its parameters. Write its probability mass function.

5. State the mean and variance of the Binomial distribution.

6. Define the Poisson distribution and state its parameter. Write its probability mass function.

7. State the mean and variance of the Poisson distribution.

8. State the condition under which the Binomial distribution can be approximated by the Poisson distribution.

9. Define the Normal distribution and state its parameters. Write its probability density function.

10. State the properties of the normal curve (shape, symmetry, area under the curve).

11. Define standard normal variate and standard normal distribution.

12. Define sampling, population, and a random sample.

13. Define a large sample test. State the general form of a large-sample test statistic (Z-test).

14. Define the null hypothesis and alternative hypothesis.

15. Define the level of significance and degrees of freedom.

16. Define the Chi-square (χ²) test for goodness of fit, and write its test statistic formula.

## Section B (7 Marks) — Intermediate

1. Find the moment generating function of the Binomial distribution. Also find the first and second moments about the mean using the MGF.

2. A manufacturer produces lightbulbs packed into boxes of 100. If 0.5% of the bulbs are defective, using an appropriate distribution, find the percentage of boxes that will contain (a) no defectives, (b) 2 or more defectives.

3. A theatre chain's customer spending is normally distributed with mean $4.11 and standard deviation $1.37. Using the standard normal table, find the percentage of customers who spend less than $3.00.

4. Using the Poisson distribution, find the probability that the ace of spades will be drawn from a well-shuffled pack of cards at least once in 104 consecutive trials.

5. The probability that a pen manufactured by a company is defective is 1/10. If 12 such pens are manufactured, find the probability that (a) exactly two will be defective, (b) at least two will be defective, (c) none will be defective.

6. A survey of 800 families with 4 children each reveals a given distribution of boys and girls. Using the Chi-square test, examine whether the result is consistent with the hypothesis that male and female births are equally probable (given the critical χ² value at the specified degrees of freedom).

7. A Council is considering whether to base a recovery vehicle on a stretch of road. Records show the average number of incidents during the morning rush hour is 5. Using the Poisson distribution, determine whether the probability of more than 5 incidents is less than 30%, and advise the Council accordingly.

8. Derive the mean and variance of the Poisson distribution from its moment generating function.

9. Two cards are drawn at random without replacement from a pack of 52 playing cards. Using the hypergeometric/classical approach, find the probability that both cards are black or both cards are spades (cross-reference with Unit III methods, but expressed via distribution reasoning).

10. Show that the Poisson distribution is a limiting case of the Binomial distribution, stating the conditions under which this approximation is valid.

## Section C (10 Marks) — Advanced

1. Find the moment generating function of the Binomial distribution. Also find the first and second moments about the mean. Use this to derive the variance formula npq.
   **OR**
   Show that the area under the normal curve is unity. Also prove that, for the normal distribution, the mean deviation from the mean is approximately equal to (4/5) of the standard deviation.

2. A survey of families with a fixed number of children each reveals a distribution of boys and girls across the families. Using the Chi-square test for goodness of fit, test the hypothesis that male and female births are equally probable, stating the null hypothesis, computing the expected frequencies, calculating χ², and stating your conclusion by comparing with the critical value.
   **OR**
   (i) Using the Poisson distribution, find the probability that a specified rare card/event occurs at least once in a large number of independent trials. (ii) The probability that an item manufactured is defective is p. If n such items are manufactured, find the probability that exactly k, at least k, and none are defective, using the Binomial distribution.

3. A Council/organization is deciding on resource allocation based on the frequency of incidents, which follow a Poisson distribution with a known average rate. Derive the probability of observing more than a specified number of incidents, and use this to make and justify a decision against a given probability threshold.
   **OR**
   Define skewness and kurtosis in the context of a probability distribution. For a distribution with given central moments, compute the coefficients of skewness and kurtosis, and relate the results to the shape parameters of an appropriate theoretical distribution (Binomial, Poisson, or Normal) that might model the data.

## How to use this set
- Section A mirrors the "2-mark," compulsory-first-part style seen in the Test-I/Test-II papers — these must be automatic; don't skip them even though they look simple.
- Section B mirrors the 3–5 mark "attempt any two/three" parts — practice picking which parts to attempt under time pressure, since real papers never require all of them.
- Section C mirrors the Major Examination's 5–10 mark "OR" full-length parts — these combine a definition/derivation with a numerical, exactly as seen in the actual paper (e.g., "define skewness and kurtosis... find the coefficients").
- Unit III (Probability) is the most PYQ-heavy unit in the actual papers — conditional probability chains (rain/traffic/late-to-work style) and Bayes' theorem with bags/urns appear in nearly every exam sitting, so prioritize repetition there.
- Cross-check Unit IV numericals against a standard normal table and a χ² critical value table — these papers routinely supply the table values you need directly in the question, so get used to reading and applying them under time pressure rather than memorizing full tables.
