Now, let’s talk about the working of a Compound DC Motor. The feedback mechanism in a DC Motor is crucial. When you provide input supply, the total current divides into two parts: one goes through the Series winding, and the other goes through the Shunt winding. 

The armature winding experiences a certain resistance, and the interaction between the field and armature plays a significant role in the motor's operation. The Series winding typically has fewer turns of thicker wire to reduce resistance, while the Shunt winding has more turns of thinner wire to increase resistance.

Let’s derive some basic relationships to understand how this works. The total potential across the motor can be expressed as the sum of the voltage drops across the armature and the field windings. 

The relationship can be expressed mathematically as follows:
\[ V = I_a \cdot R_a + I_f \cdot R_f \]
where \( V \) is the supply voltage, \( I_a \) is the armature current, \( R_a \) is the armature resistance, \( I_f \) is the field current, and \( R_f \) is the field resistance.

In a Long Shunt DC Motor, the Series winding is connected in parallel with the Shunt winding. This configuration allows for a combination of high starting torque and good speed regulation. 

It is important to note that a pure Series DC Motor provides higher starting torque compared to a Compound DC Motor. However, the Compound DC Motor can regulate its speed better than a Series Motor.
